# The Tolerated Pain

A point of view on the enterprise AI opportunity nobody is repricing: the backlog of problems every organization already decided to live with.

**By Mathew Baldwin, [@ungatedbluemarble](https://github.com/ungatedbluemarble)**

---

## What this is

Every enterprise carries a portfolio of known problems it has chosen to tolerate. These are not oversights. They are rational decisions made under an old cost basis, where fixing a problem meant a project, a budget line, an engineering team, and quarters of waiting, so the friction was cheaper than the cure. This piece argues that the cost basis underneath a large class of those decisions has quietly collapsed, because the capability to close language-and-workflow problems now ships inside the AI licenses organizations already pay for. The real opportunity is not a moonshot. It is repricing the backlog already chosen for tolerance.

The argument is disciplined about what qualifies. It draws a hard line between skill-shaped problems that close through configuration in an afternoon and system-shaped problems that still require real engineering and a roadmap slot, and it treats that judgment as the scarce skill. It answers the governance objection directly: the sprawl of unsanctioned tools is already here, gatekeeping has already lost, and the durable control is standardization by promotion, taking one person's working solve and hardening it into an owned, maintained departmental standard. And it locates why this stopped being optional, in the EU AI Act and the wave of AI governance it is setting the template for, where the first requirement is the complete, classified, owned inventory of AI in use that most organizations cannot yet produce.

This repository holds the written argument and the supporting diagrams. It is a point of view, not a product, and it is grounded in public law and public sources.
